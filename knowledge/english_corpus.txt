English is a language used by people to share ideas, ask questions, tell stories, and explain the world. People learn language by listening, reading, speaking, writing, practising, and receiving feedback. A word has a form and one or more meanings. The meaning of a word depends on the sentence and the situation in which it is used.

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought. In English, a basic sentence often follows subject, verb, and object order. The subject tells who or what the sentence is about. The verb tells an action or state. The object can receive the action. For example, the student reads a book. The student is the subject, reads is the verb, and a book is the object.

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. A verb expresses an action, event, or state. An adjective describes a noun. An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb. A pronoun can replace a noun phrase. A preposition expresses a relationship such as place, time, or direction. A conjunction connects words or clauses.

English verbs change form to express tense, agreement, and other grammatical information. The present simple can describe habits and general truths. The past simple can describe completed past events. The future can be expressed with will and other constructions. The verb be has the forms am, is, and are in the present tense. I am, you are, he is, she is, it is, we are, and they are.

Subject and verb should agree. She reads every day. They read every day. He is ready. We are ready. I am learning English. After does not, use the base form of the verb: She does not like tea. Do not say she does not likes tea. The articles a and an are used with singular countable nouns. Use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound.

Questions in English often use a question word such as who, what, where, when, why, or how. Yes or no questions often use an auxiliary verb. Do you understand? Is she ready? Where do they live? A question mark is used at the end of a direct question.

Punctuation helps readers understand written language. A full stop ends a statement. A question mark ends a direct question. A comma can separate items in a list or parts of a sentence. A capital letter begins a sentence and is used for names and the pronoun I.

Learning requires examples and practice. A language learner should compare sentences, notice patterns, test a rule, and correct mistakes. One example is useful, but many varied examples are better. A dictionary explains words, but a dictionary alone does not teach all grammar, meaning, context, or reasoning. A language system needs both vocabulary and examples of how words are used.

A good conversation involves more than matching words. A speaker considers the other person's question, the previous messages, the topic, and the likely purpose. When information is missing, asking a clarifying question is better than inventing an answer. A system should communicate uncertainty honestly.

The word affect is usually a verb meaning to influence. The word effect is usually a noun meaning a result. A curious student asks questions. A careful reader checks the meaning of a word in context. A clear explanation uses simple language and relevant examples.
